# Framing calibration — worked examples (2a vs 2b)

Step 2 splits the framing check into two independent sub-checks: 2a (scope currency) and 2b (practice vs. spec). The two tests sound similar in the abstract, and 2b is the one that's easy to skip in favor of the easier, more searchable 2a — this file exists to anchor the distinction with a real worked example so a borderline finding gets filed under the right sub-check, and so 2b keeps getting genuinely attempted rather than nodded at. When a new review surfaces a finding that's hard to place, add a worked example here rather than just re-explaining the abstract test better — same philosophy as severity-calibration.md.

**The distinguishing test:** does the fix mean "add/update to reflect something newer that current sources treat as core" (2a), or "point out that the piece's advice, while valid, isn't the operational default real teams reach for first" (2b)? A missing category, capability, or mechanism is almost always 2a. A recommendation that's technically sound but not what practitioners actually configure by default is 2b — the piece can be fully current on 2a and still fail 2b.

## Worked example: a "good bot vs. bad bot" explainer (2026)

The same piece produced one clean finding for each sub-check, which is the ideal illustration of why they need to be checked and reported separately rather than merged into one verdict.

**2a (scope currency) — ISSUE.** The piece's bot taxonomy (search / AI-training / AI-search / commercial-scraper) omits the "Agent" / "Autonomous Agent" transactional-bot category that Cloudflare and DataDome formalized in 2026 (Cloudflare blocks this category by default on ad-supported pages starting a stated date; DataDome's category was renamed by Forrester to "Bot and Agent Trust Management"). The piece also frames cryptographic bot verification (Web Bot Auth, built on RFC 9421) as a future hypothetical ("adoption gains ground... in the meantime") when it's already live production infrastructure with a default-block timeline. Both are pure currency gaps — the piece is describing a domain picture from roughly a year before the search results that contradict it. **Test applied:** the fix is "this capability/category now exists and the piece doesn't mention it" — that's 2a.

**2b (practice vs. spec) — ISSUE, and a distinct finding from the 2a one above.** The piece's recommended handling for ambiguous bot traffic is a bespoke "graylisting" approach: a custom cryptographic challenge plus custom log analysis. Nothing about this is outdated or technically wrong — it would work, and current sources don't contradict it. But it's not what practitioners actually do first: current default guidance across vendors is to simply enable the CDN's built-in verified-bots/agent toggle, a coarser off-the-shelf default that requires no custom build. The piece never mentions that simpler default at all, jumping straight to the advanced custom approach as if it were the standard first move. **Test applied:** the fix isn't "this is stale," it's "this is a real approach that isn't the operational default" — that's 2b.

**Why both had to be checked, and why one wouldn't have caught the other:** a search aimed at 2a ("what's the current bot taxonomy," "what's new in bot management 2026") would never surface the graylisting-vs-toggle gap, because the graylisting approach isn't wrong or dated by that search's own standard — it just isn't what operators reach for first. Finding it required the adversarial 2b-style search instead: "what do security teams actually configure for bot management by default," not "what's newest."
